\begin{abstract}
R08 addresses the three items R07 left open. (2) \textbf{Gram-reduced count: CLOSED.} Exactly one strictly-4D Gram
identity can act at this order: the $5\times5$ Gram determinant $G$ of $\{a_1,a_2,a_3,p_1,p_2\}$ in the $X_H$ sector. It is
a Bose-symmetric cocycle with a vanishing $\bar\sigma$ image, so the three-point rows do not see it (it is blind); it is
not trivial. Hence $\dim\bar H_{\rm Gram}=\HBg=\dim\bar H_{\rm formal}$; blind goes $24\to\BGg$ and $\dim\bar N_g$ goes
$133\to\NBg$. (3) \textbf{Parity-odd: partial.} All inputs are parity-even and the odd sector decouples with zero

no DRED $\epsilon$ calculus; it is not zero. (1) \textbf{Four-point rows: OPEN.} The required vertices and loop poles do
not exist in the frozen calculus. Nothing is assumed zero. Outcome: \textbf{Partial}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Front 2: Gram-reduced scheme}
The R06/R07 count is made in the formal $H/T$ algebra. A Gram-reduced scheme imposes the identity
$\det[\hat v_i\cdot\hat v_j]_{5\times5}=0$, so every such combination becomes evanescent.

\textbf{Which identities can act.} A Gram determinant has 10 vector slots, five rows and five columns. A $T$-containing
monomial keeps at most $(\text{slots}-2)$ hat slots. The slot counts are $X_K$: 8, $X_C$: 8, $X_S$: 6, $X_H$: 10; the
rows have $\le10$ slots, with at most 8 hat slots in their $T$-containing terms. So Gram identities touch only pure-$H$
structures, and the only candidate is $X_H$ ($a_1^2a_2^2a_3^2$, momentum degree 4). There each $a_i$ must occur once in
the rows and once in the columns, and $p_1,p_2$ likewise, since a repeated vector kills the determinant. This leaves the
unique polynomial
$$G=\det[\hat v_i\cdot\hat v_j],\qquad v=(a_1,a_2,a_3,p_1,p_2),$$
which has \GT{} terms and lies in the 4D basis. For the hhc row (multidegree $c\,a_2^2a_3^2$, momentum 5), any candidate
determinant would repeat a momentum, so there is no Gram relation among the rows.

\textbf{Properties.} $G$ is Bose symmetric: leg swaps act on $\mathrm{span}(p_1,p_2)$ with determinant $\pm1$, which
enters squared. Its $\bar\sigma$ image vanishes, because channel $A$ replaces $a_1\to\mathrm{sym}(p_1,c)$, which repeats
$p_1$ in a row or column. $G$ does not enter the ghost rows or the ghost equation.

\textbf{Count.} We add $G$ as one evanescent coordinate and repeat R07's complete-$Y$ classification under the full hat
rule. A trivial direction is now any $(EV\,z,\lambda)$ with $FD\,z=\lambda\,g$, i.e.\ a 4D remainder proportional to $G$.
R07 is reproduced: $\bar N_g=133$, $T=126$, blind $=24$, $\bar H=6$. In the Gram scheme $\bar N_g=\NBg$, $T=\TBg$, blind
$=\BGg$, and $\bar H=\HBg$. $G$ is blind and not trivial. \textbf{The count is scheme-stable: $\dim\bar H=6$ in both the
formal and the Gram-reduced scheme} (disclosure R08-D1).

\section{Front 3: parity-odd sector}
Every input is built from $H$, $T$ and loop $s$ dots only. This covers the stored Q05--Q07 loop poles, the R07 reduced
residual, the counterterm and the p22 completion, and it was checked symbol by symbol. The frozen calculus has no
Levi-Civita object. $\bar\sigma$ acts by vector substitutions and metric contractions, which preserve the number of
$\epsilon$ tensors mod 2. Therefore:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the odd sector decouples from the even one;
\item its inhomogeneity is zero, so $X_{\rm odd}=0$ is a consistent choice and the even results stand unchanged;
\item the dimension of the odd evanescent cohomology requires a DRED $\epsilon$ (a $Q4S$ $\epsilon$ with tilde
components) and its row maps. These are Missing, and the dimension is not zero by default.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Outcome: PARTIAL} (R08-D2).

\section{Front 1: four-point rows}
The frozen calculus has three-point vertices only. It lacks the $\kappa^2$ four-graviton vertex, the $\tilde Kchh$ and
$\bar cchh$ vertices, the one-loop four-point UV poles for the rows $chhh$, $cc\bar ch$ and $ccKh$, and a four-point
evanescent ansatz with its $\bar\sigma$ map. Without these, neither the extension of the six classes to four points nor
the four-point inhomogeneity can be decided. \textbf{Outcome: OPEN} (R08-D3). The six classes are therefore classes of the
three-point linearised problem; whether they extend to four points is unestablished.
